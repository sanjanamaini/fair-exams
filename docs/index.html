Fair Exams Explorer

NEET-UG 2024, the only year the National Testing Agency published every candidate's marks centre by centre (by Supreme Court order). Every centre is tested against the rest of its own city.

A flag means a centre's results are unlikely if candidates were allotted to centres at random within the city. It is a reason to review, never evidence of misconduct: a stronger cohort allotted together (by paper medium, school or registration batch) produces the same signal.

Start with: R.K. University Unit-1, Rajkot (the one centre on both lists) · Oasis School, Hazaribagh (where the 2024 paper leaked) · Jay Jalaram School, Godhra (a foiled plan) · Sikar (the largest hub)

    What each number means

    Expected: top-1% scorers the centre would have, on average, if its candidates were a random draw from its city. z (adjusted): how far the actual count sits from that, in standard errors, after widening for how non-random allotment is nationally (the Kish design effect). q: false discovery rate across all 4,656 centres tested; flags are q of 0.05 or less. Tail beyond the bulk: whether the centre has more top scorers than its own middle half predicts. A stronger cohort shifts the whole distribution; a boost to some candidates shifts only the top. Stability: share of 50 random seeds that flag the centre (the tests use randomised p-values).

    Single-centre cities cannot be tested within the city. Leaks sold before the exam to candidates spread across a city are invisible to any within-city test; they need between-city comparison or item-level answer-similarity analysis, which only NTA can run.
